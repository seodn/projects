### Main Topic

Initial redesign and SEO/AI-search direction.

### Key Decisions

- The new website should be more detailed because Google and AI search behaviour has changed.
- The website needs more answer-led sections, not only traditional "childcare centre + suburb" wording.
- Client preferred "quality childcare in Auckland" over "best childcare in Auckland" because "best" sounds like a self-claim.
- Pricing should be handled carefully.
- Exact fees should not be listed publicly.
- A generic childcare cost blog or FAQ is acceptable.
- The site should answer practical parent questions about safety, affordability, quality, subsidies, transport, and meals.
- The design direction was generally liked but fonts needed improvement.

### SEO/AI Search Notes

Search behaviour discussed:

- parents are moving from simple searches like "childcare centre East Tamaki" to fuller questions like "what is the safest childcare centre for my child that is affordable and delivers quality?"
- site should include FAQs and answer-led sections for AI search extraction
- licensed/safe environment, philosophy, costs, age groups, and parent journey should be clearly explained

### Pricing Notes

- Client does not want exact fee tables online.
- Fees vary by centre, age, hours, occupancy, and offer.
- General pricing ranges can be explained in a guide.
- A Nature's Point-style "childcare costs in Auckland" article was discussed as a model.
- Promotions can vary by centre and occupancy.
- A 50% discount at Dannemora was discussed at the time, but this must be confirmed before publishing.

### Parent Journey Notes

Typical enquiry journey:

1. Parent finds the website or calls.
2. Staff encourage the parent to visit the centre.
3. Staff discuss current offers, childcare subsidy, FamilyBoost, and family eligibility.
4. Staff explain documentation and support parents where possible.
5. Transport and food can be key practical support points.

### Mission

Core mission:

- provide quality childcare and education
- create a home-away-from-home environment for babies
- challenge toddlers so they reach potential
- support preschoolers with a strong transition-to-school programme

### Parent Trust And Differentiation

Why parents choose Little Climbers:

- quality childcare at a competitive/affordable price
- friendly staff
- approachable teachers
- homely centres
- small-centre environment
- direct access to staff and management
- open-door policy
- personal relationships
- multicultural teaching team can help children settle where language/culture is a concern

Parent worries:

- safety

- first-time parents worry about leaving their child

### Child Development FAQs

Starting age:

- Children can start from around 3 months where the centre offers infant care.
- The client does not want to publicly say that children "should" stay home for a year, as that can make parents feel judged or suggest the centre cannot support babies.
- Public answer should say the right age depends on family needs, work, the child's stage, and centre availability.

Social development:

- Daycare supports social development.
- Children learn to make friends, settle in a new environment, communicate, and adjust.

School readiness:

- transition-to-school programme
- focus on social skills, communication, self-help skills, emotional expression, looking after belongings
- not only reading/writing

Screen time:

- Ritu's view: zero is ideal, not more than 30 minutes a day.
- Use carefully if turned into a blog.

Separation anxiety:

- parents should spend a little time at drop-off
- say goodbye clearly rather than disappearing
- explain they are going to work and will return
- transition visits help
- some children settle quickly, others take longer

Signs a child has settled:

- looks forward to seeing friends/teachers
- talks about the centre at home
- shares stories from home at the centre

### Choosing Childcare FAQs

What parents should look for:

- safe environment
- resources
- teacher interaction
- curriculum
- routines
- menu
- transition process
- ratios, especially for babies

High quality is subjective:

- parents have different expectations
- some want early reading/writing, others want fun and social development
- Little Climbers should explain its play-based, whole-child approach clearly
