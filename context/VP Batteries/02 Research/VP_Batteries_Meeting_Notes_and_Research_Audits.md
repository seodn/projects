# Client Meeting: Cleaned Notes

These notes preserve the meeting’s meaning while removing transcription noise. They are not finished website copy.

## Founder story

●        Vaughan previously worked in hospitality and wanted to move away from nights, weekends and public holidays.

●        His father worked in vehicle sales and helped him get his first interview in the battery industry.

●        Vaughan has worked in the battery industry for about 28 years across several companies.

●        He believed customers could receive good-quality products at fairer prices and with better service.

●        He started VP Batteries around 2020, shortly before the COVID disruption.

●        VP stands for Vaughan Perry.

## Business model

●        VP Batteries serves wholesale, trade, retail and mobile customers.

●        Vaughan’s principle is that a customer buying one battery should be treated as well as a customer buying 100.

●        Trade generates more revenue, but the public and trade audiences should both feel welcome.

●        The business tries to source uncommon products rather than ending the conversation with “we don’t have it”.

●        Workshops, car yards and other businesses can receive trade pricing.

●        Mechanics and workshops.

●        Car yards.

●        Fleet and rental operators.

●        Trucking and bus companies.

●        Boat owners and boat companies.

●        Campervan and motorhome owners.

●        Farmers.

●        Earthmoving and industrial businesses.

## Mobile service

●        Callouts may be to a customer’s home, workplace, supermarket car park or roadside location.

●        The mobile service was described as Canterbury-wide.

●        Products can be supplied more broadly across the South Island.

●        The business has advertised 24/7 and Vaughan tries to help urgent customers, but availability is personal and not unconditional.

●        Most fitting attached to a battery purchase was described as free.

●        A test-only or jump-start callout was described as $50.

●        Complex or lengthy fitting may attract a charge.

## Product and brand notes

●        Hyundai batteries manufactured in Korea were described as a main product source.

●        Other brands are held for AGM, deep-cycle, local replenishment and gaps in the main range.

●        All retail products were described as new. VP Batteries does not present used batteries as “refurbished”.

●        The team keeps learning as vehicle electrical systems change.

●        VP Batteries can help with a hybrid or EV vehicle’s 12-volt battery but not the high-voltage traction pack.

## Selection and testing

●        A common mistake is buying the wrong battery type based only on size or assumption.

●        Stop-start vehicles generally require a suitable EFB or AGM battery.

●        A normal battery installed in a stop-start application may have a shortened life and may not satisfy warranty conditions.

●        Vehicle details should be collected before recommending a product.

●        Phone advice alone cannot always distinguish a flat battery, dead cell, alternator fault or starter issue.

●        With the vehicle present, VP Batteries can test battery condition, alternator output and starter performance.

●        A flat battery may need charging before a reliable condition test.

●        Workshop testing and charging were described as free.

## Battery-life and care advice

●        The meeting referenced a typical New Zealand battery life of three to five years, while Vaughan believes a good battery should often achieve around five years.

●        This should be written as guidance, not a guarantee.

●        Weak batteries can affect modern vehicle systems, including steering, transmission behaviour, ABS warnings and general electrical operation.

●        Vehicles used infrequently or only for short trips may benefit from a maintenance or trickle charger.

●        Keeping a battery properly charged helps reduce sulphation and internal resistance.

## Warranty discussion

●        Retail automotive: generally three years.

●        Commercial: often 24 months, with some at 12 months.

●        Taxi: six months.

●        These statements conflict with parts of the workbook and need a final schedule.

## Recycling

●        VP Batteries accepts old batteries for recycling, including units not bought from the business.

●        The business may collect or buy old batteries from other companies.

●        Vaughan described responsible supplier production and proper disposal as part of “working for a greener future”.

●        The recycling destination was uncertain and must not be published.

## Brand impression

The client wants people to remember:

1.    Service.

2.    Positive attitude.

3.    Good pricing.

The brand should be both trade-focused and public-friendly.

## Executive finding

The site is already running WordPress and WooCommerce. Public assets and URLs show WooCommerce product, category, shop, cart, checkout, registration, account and wishlist functions.

The practical first option is to audit and finish the existing installation on a staging copy. A full platform rebuild is unnecessary unless the hosting, theme, plugins, security or maintainability check fails.

## Critical content and trust issues

1.    Product pages contain placeholder Lorem Ipsum.

2.    The returns content on a product page contains a San Francisco return address.

3.    Some copy contains visible errors, including “mooo”, spelling mistakes and duplicated words.

4.    “More than 20 years” is out of date against Vaughan’s current approximately 28 years.

5.    “Guarantee all our products” is too broad without the written warranty policy.

6.    Some product images and descriptions appear mismatched.

7.    Product pages show specifications but many do not show a price or purchase action.

8.    Some page and product-category URLs target the same topic, creating duplication risk.

9.    Navigation and content are duplicated in the rendered output.

10.  The public pages do not explain the actual retail, trade, mobile and after-hours rules clearly.

## Ecommerce gaps

●        Cart and checkout URLs exist, but an end-to-end live payment journey was not confirmed.

●        Product prices and “add to cart” are absent from the inspected product page.

●        Shipping, fitting and local delivery rules are not clear.

●        The public returns wording is unsafe to use.

●        Fitment confirmation is not sufficiently prominent.

●        Trade pricing and credit-account handling are not defined.

●        Stock accuracy and ownership are not documented.

## SEO gaps

●        Product pages and category archives need one clear keyword owner per topic.

●        Cart, checkout, account, register and wishlist should not be indexable.

●        Core service pages need stronger direct answers and local proof.

●        The site should separate “mobile battery replacement” from general battery retail.

●        Trade and wholesale deserve a dedicated page.

●        The internal link structure should connect advice, service and relevant product categories.

## Recommended technical path

1.    Back up the live site and database.

2.    Create a password-protected staging copy.

3.    Record theme, plugins, PHP version, WordPress/WooCommerce versions, hosting and licences.

4.    Check security, performance and plugin conflicts.

5.    Clean product data and policies before enabling payments.

6.    Build the approved information architecture on staging.

7.    Preserve strong URLs and create one-to-one redirects for retired or merged pages.

8.    Run test orders, emails, stock changes, refunds and mobile flows.

9.    QA forms, tracking, schema, indexation, mobile speed and accessibility.

10.  Launch only after Vaughan signs off content, prices and policies.

●        San Francisco returns address.

●        Unsupported guarantees.

●        Unapproved customer reviews or market-leading claims.

●        Duplicate or incorrect products.

●        Any payment-logo display that does not match the enabled gateway.
