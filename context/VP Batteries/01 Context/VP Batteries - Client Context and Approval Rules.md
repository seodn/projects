VP Batteries Client Brief

Business

VP Batteries is a Christchurch-owned battery supplier founded by Vaughan Perry around 2020. “VP” stands for Vaughan Perry.

Vaughan entered the battery industry after working in hospitality. His father, who worked in vehicle sales, helped him secure his first battery-industry interview. Vaughan has approximately 28 years of battery-industry experience. He started VP Batteries to offer good-quality products, fairer pricing and more personal service.

Murray is part of the team. The meeting indicates that he previously spent about six years with Exide Batteries and has around ten years of battery-industry experience in total. His exact title and surname still need confirmation.

Commercial model

VP Batteries serves:

●        trade and wholesale customers;

●        mechanical workshops and garages;

●        car yards and vehicle-rental businesses;

●        trucking, bus, earthmoving, farm and industrial operators;

●        boat, campervan and motorhome owners;

●        everyday drivers and other retail buyers;

●        customers who need mobile delivery, testing or fitting.

Trade customers currently contribute more revenue than retail customers. The website must not hide this trade strength behind a retail-only online shop.

Customer promise

The strongest defensible promise is:

Practical battery help from experienced Christchurch owners who will ask the right questions, test before replacing where appropriate, find suitable products and help customers get moving.

Primary goals

1.    Increase calls and qualified battery enquiries.

2.    Make mobile battery replacement easy to request.

3.    Strengthen trade and wholesale lead generation.

4.    Help customers choose the correct battery rather than buying by guesswork.

5.    Turn suitable products into safe online purchases.

6.    Build Christchurch and Canterbury visibility in Google and AI answers.

7.    Replace incomplete, outdated and placeholder content on the current site.

Customer problems to solve

●        The vehicle will not start and the customer does not know whether the battery is flat, faulty or affected by the alternator or starter.

●        The customer cannot get the vehicle to the workshop.

●        The buyer does not know the correct battery type or fitment.

●        A workshop or fleet needs dependable supply, trade pricing and someone who will help source uncommon products.

●        A customer has an old lead-acid battery that needs responsible recycling.

●        A modern stop-start, hybrid or EV vehicle needs the correct 12-volt battery type.

Differentiators

●        Owner-operated, Christchurch-based service.

●        Approximately 28 years of Vaughan’s industry experience.

●        Murray’s additional battery experience.

●        Retail, trade, wholesale and mobile service in one business.

●        Free workshop battery testing, subject to final terms.

●        Battery, alternator and starter checks when the vehicle is present.

●        Willingness to source products that are not normally held.

●        Fitting for vehicles the team can safely and competently access.

●        Old lead-acid battery collection and recycling.

Important scope clarification

VP Batteries can supply 12-volt batteries used by some hybrid and electric vehicles. The business does not repair or replace high-voltage EV traction battery packs.

Workshop testing

When a vehicle is present, VP Batteries can:

●        check alternator charging voltage;

●        test whether the battery is holding charge;

●        identify signs of a dead cell or an old, tired battery;

●        check starter performance;

●        charge a flat battery before retesting when a reliable test is not initially possible.

The meeting says workshop testing and, where needed, charging are free. Reconfirm the conditions.

Fitting

The meeting says most fitting is free when a battery is purchased, but complex or time-consuming fitting may be charged. Do not advertise universal free fitting.

●        The business may collect or buy old batteries from other businesses.

Content Rules

Core rules

●        Write for a buyer who may be stranded, unsure of fitment or ordering for a business.

●        Put the action near the top: call, request mobile help, find a battery or open a trade account.

●        Ask for vehicle make, model, year, engine, registration and start-stop status when fitment matters.

●        Explain AGM, EFB, CCA and amp-hours only where the explanation helps selection.

●        Use Christchurch as the primary location signal. Use Canterbury for mobile coverage only after boundaries are approved.

●        Distinguish workshop testing from paid mobile callouts.

●        Distinguish product delivery from mobile fitting.

●        Distinguish retail pricing from trade pricing.

●        Distinguish a 12-volt EV auxiliary battery from the high-voltage traction pack.

SEO and AI-search rules

●        Each page owns one primary intent.

●        Do not create both a normal page and a product-category archive for the same keyword unless their roles are deliberately different.

●        Product pages should include a concise compatibility warning and a vehicle-confirmation CTA.

●        Answer common questions directly in 40 to 60 words, then expand.

●        Use real named expertise, approved specifications and policy details as evidence.

●        Add FAQ schema only for visible FAQs.

●        Add LocalBusiness/AutomotiveBusiness, Product, Breadcrumb and Organisation schema only where fields are accurate.

●        Keep cart, checkout, account, registration and wishlist utility pages out of search results.

Conversion rules

●        Header CTAs: Call now, Find a battery, Trade account.

●        Mobile users must be able to call in one tap.

●        Do not make an urgent buyer search through the shop before seeing the mobile option.

●        Do not force a trade buyer through retail checkout.

●        Display price components clearly: battery, fitting, callout, delivery, GST and any old-battery/core condition.

●        Show workshop hours separately from after-hours call availability.
